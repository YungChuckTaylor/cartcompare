Can the Datafiniti free tier carry a cart-compare MVP?

Short answer: you can absolutely build the MVP on it this week, and I have. You cannot launch a Canadian cart comparison on it — and the reason is not the price.

Yes, buildableA working cart-compare UI, basket maths and a swappable Datafiniti adapter already run in this repo, at $0.
No, not shippableIt is a 14-day trial, not a free tier. Canadian grocery coverage is close to zero. The licence blocks republishing prices.

1It is not a free tier. It is a 14-day trial of 1,000 records.

This is the single most important correction to make, because "free tier" implies a floor you can run on forever. There isn't one.

The published product-data card reads “Trial — 1k free product records, free for 2 weeks.” After 14 days the trial ends and the cheapest paid tier is $99/month. That is not a free tier with limits; that is a demo allowance with an expiry date.

The billing unit is the record, not the request

Every call returns num_found (free) and total_cost (what you spent). A returned record is one credit. num_records is capped at 10 for a normal search. So the arithmetic that matters:

1,000 records ÷ 10 records per barcode lookup = 100 lookups. One 12-line cart, tested eight times, and the trial is gone.

You can stretch it — ask for one record instead of ten, cache aggressively, and never re-query the same barcode — but you cannot stretch 1,000 records into a product that strangers use.

The one trick worth knowing num_found is free. You can ask "how many records do you have for this query?" for the price of a single returned record. That turns the trial from a data allowance into a coverage measurement instrument — which is the best possible use of it. Section 4 is exactly that, and it costs about 8 credits.

2Coverage is US-first, and grocery is the weak spot

Even if the trial were unlimited, this is the wall you hit. Datafiniti's own product page says so plainly.

Their words, not mine

"Coverage primarily focuses on the U.S. Some international data may appear, but is not guaranteed at scale." Your own provider census already reached the same conclusion: 70,000+ merchants including Canadian storefronts, "though Canadian depth is a fraction of US depth."

There is a second problem underneath the geography, and it is the one that kills a cart product specifically. Datafiniti builds its product database by crawling e-commerce product listing pages. That means:

  • No shelf prices. What you get is what a web page displayed, not what the tag under the product said.
  • No flyer or promo context. There is an isSale flag, but not the regular-vs-promo pair that makes "is this a deal?" answerable.
  • The Canadian grocery banners are the hardest to crawl and the least likely to be in the index. No Frills, Food Basics, FreshCo, Metro and Sobeys all sit behind bot protection, Instacart, or Voilà storefronts.
  • A cart is a basket, not a product. Comparing baskets requires the same UPC at five merchants. Any per-product coverage number you measure gets raised to the power of your cart size. 60% per-item coverage over an 8-line cart is 1.7% of carts fully comparable.
The realistic Canadian picture: Datafiniti will give you Walmart.ca, Amazon.ca and Best Buy Canada-shaped general merchandise. It will not give you a grocery basket across five banners. That is an electronics-shopping product, not a cart compare.

3The licence clause is the quiet blocker

If your product is essentially republishing prices, this is the clause your business depends on.

ClauseWhat it saysWhy it matters to you
§3May not resell or sublicense the data "without significant modifications" Undefined test Showing a price next to a merchant name is arguably not a significant modification. Get your specific use in writing before you pay.
§6On termination, delete all Datafiniti data and certify it Dependency Your catalogue disappears with the subscription. Not an asset you own.
§7May suspend or throttle services at its sole discretion Availability risk A price app that can be throttled at will is not infrastructure.
§5Price list may change at any time, no refund for prior charges Normal Budget for it, don't build a unit-economics story on a fixed rate.

None of this is fatal for a prototype. All of it is fatal for "Datafiniti is my price layer."

4Spend 8 credits, not 1,000, to get the real answer

I built the measurement so you don't have to guess. This is the decision gate that should happen before you ever enter a card.

# see exactly what will be asked, spend nothing
npm run probe:dry

# run 8 probes against the live API: ~8 credits, ~15 seconds
npm run probe

It asks eight questions, one record each, and prints num_found for every one. The sixth and seventh are the ones that decide your product:

Prediction, so you can hold me to it Probes 1 and 2 will return a number. Probes 3 and 5 will return zero or near-zero. Probe 6 (electronics) will return something. If that is what happens, Datafiniti is a US electronics catalogue available to a Canadian founder — useful for a cross-border price product, useless for a grocery cart.

5What I built instead: the MVP, with Datafiniti as one adapter

The point of the architecture is that today's answer being "no" doesn't cost you the work.

The repo now runs a complete cart comparison at http://localhost:4173. It works with no API key at all, because the data layer is behind an interface and the demo provider is not a mock — it is a real provider that returns the same shape Datafiniti does.

FileWhat it doesWhy it's shaped that way
src/datafiniti.jsDatafiniti v4 client, query builder, record normaliser The only file that knows Datafiniti exists
src/demo.jsSynthetic catalogue provider Lets the product be built and demoed at $0
src/prices.jsProvider choice, cache, credit budget Swap the price source here, nothing else moves
src/compare.jsBasket maths: cheapest per merchant, totals, coverage Pure functions, unit tested, provider-agnostic
scripts/probe-coverage.jsThe 8-credit coverage measurement Turn the trial into evidence instead of data
public/Cart builder, basket cards, item-by-item matrix No build step, no framework, no npm install

Two product decisions the MVP takes seriously

  1. A partial basket is never presented as a cheap basket. A store that carries 2 of your 5 lines will show a lower total than a store that carries all 5. The UI badges coverage on every card and offers a "only stores carrying the whole cart" filter. This is the mistake every comparison app makes, and it is the fastest way to lose a user's trust.
  2. Region is a first-class filter, not a setting. "Canadian offers only" is on by default, and offers with no Canadian evidence are visibly separated rather than silently mixed in — so the coverage gap is the first thing you see, not the last thing you discover.
Why this matters for your budget Every hour spent on the cart UI, the basket maths, the coverage-honest ranking and the empty states is permanently reusable, no matter which provider wins. The provider integration is roughly 200 lines. Build the product, rent the data.

7What to do this week

  1. Run the probe before anything elsenpm run probe with a trial account. Eight credits, fifteen seconds, and you will know more than any vendor call will tell you.
  2. Walk through the MVP with the demo dataAdd five products, watch the basket cards. The question "does the cart comparison feel like a product?" is answerable today, for free, and it is the question that actually decides whether this is worth building.
  3. Pick the category from the probe, not from the planIf Canadian electronics is covered and grocery is not, the MVP is an electronics comparison. That is a smaller business, but it is a real one.
  4. Get the licence clause answered in writingAsk specifically: "may we display individual product prices to consumers, attributed to the merchant, with a link to the source listing?" That sentence is the whole business. Do not accept a salesperson's paraphrase.
  5. Do not pay $99 to find outPay $99 only after probe results and a demo user session both say the data is there. The trial exists precisely so you don't have to buy the answer.
The version of this that is actually true Datafiniti is a legitimate, fairly priced, well-documented product — the census was right to flag it as the most interesting new vendor. It is just the wrong shape for a Canadian grocery cart MVP, and the word "free" is doing more work in that question than the data is.
