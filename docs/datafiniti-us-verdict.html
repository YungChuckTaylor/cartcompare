1What the trial actually is

Not a free tier. A metered, expiring evaluation allowance — and in the US, a genuinely useful one.

The published card reads “Trial — 1k free product records, free for 2 weeks.” After 14 days the cheapest paid tier is $99/month for 5,000 records. Billing is per record returned: num_records caps at 10 per call, and each returned record costs one credit.

The plan that actually fits inside 1,000 records

A naive build burns the trial in an afternoon: one 12-line cart, re-rendered eight times, is 960 records. But you do not have to work that way. Cache by barcode and the trial becomes a one-time seed:

Curate ~40 SKUs. Query each once. Cache permanently. 40 × 10 records = 400 credits for the whole catalogue, and every subsequent visit is free. That is a demoable US cart comparison for $0.

The bill arrives when you want open-ended search — a user typing any product and getting an answer. That is the point where $99/month becomes honest, and by then you should know whether the cart comparison is a product.

The free measurement num_found costs nothing. You can ask "how many US records match this query?" for the price of a single returned record. That turns the trial from a data allowance into a coverage instrument — which is exactly what Section 4 does for nine credits.

2Coverage: the US edition

This is where the pivot pays off. In Canada, coverage was the fatal blocker. Here it is the argument for the vendor.

What their own page promises

70,000+ merchants, 250M+ product records, "coverage primarily focuses on the U.S." For a US cart MVP, being US-first is the entire point. Walmart, Target, Amazon, Best Buy and the major grocery e-commerce surfaces are the merchants a US shopper actually compares.

Two caveats that are real but manageable:

  • It is an online-price product, not a shelf-price product. Records are crawled from e-commerce listing pages. You get what Walmart.com showed, not what the tag in aisle 7 said. For a US audience that is mostly fine — online grocery pickup is mainstream, and "Walmart.com vs Target.com" is a question people genuinely ask. It is not fine if your pitch is "what's the real in-store price".
  • Basket coverage compounds against you. A cart needs the same UPC at five merchants. Per-item coverage raised to cart size is the only coverage number that matters: 60% per item over 8 lines is 1.7% of carts fully comparable. Measure merchant spread per SKU, not catalogue size.
What will still be wrong Costco and other membership clubs, in-store-only promotions, and fresh/perishable grocery weights and measures. If your user's question is "is this a good price today, in this store", Datafiniti is a reference layer, not the answer. Be explicit about that in the UI — this MVP badges every basket with its coverage for exactly this reason.

3The licence clause is the remaining blocker

Coverage stopped being a problem. This did not.

ClauseWhat it saysWhy it matters to you
§3May not resell or sublicense the data "without significant modifications" Undefined test Displaying a price next to a merchant name is arguably not a significant modification. Get your exact use in writing before you pay.
§6On termination, delete all Datafiniti data and certify it Dependency Your catalogue disappears with the subscription. Rent, not own.
§7May suspend or throttle services at its sole discretion Availability A price app that can be throttled at will is not infrastructure.
§5Price list may change at any time, no refund for prior charges Normal Do not build unit economics on a fixed rate.

The sentence to send them, in writing, before any card is entered: “May we display individual product prices to consumers, attributed to the merchant, alongside a link to the source listing, in a free consumer web application?” That single answer decides whether this is a business or a prototype.

4Spend 9 credits, not 1,000, to get the real answer

The measurement is built. Run it before you commit budget.

5What has been built: a US MVP, deployed

The architecture means today's answer being "yes, with conditions" did not have to be proven before the product existed.

CartCompare now runs as a US product on Vercel: build a basket from a 12-product catalogue spanning nine US retailers, and it returns ranked basket totals plus an item-by-item price matrix. It runs with no API key at all, because the data layer is behind an interface and the demo provider returns the same shape Datafiniti does.

FileWhat it doesWhy it's shaped that way
src/datafiniti.jsDatafiniti v4 client, query builder, record normaliser The only file that knows Datafiniti exists
src/demo.jsSynthetic US catalogue provider Lets the product be built, demoed and shipped at $0
src/prices.jsProvider choice, cache, credit budget Swap the price source here, nothing else moves
src/compare.jsBasket maths: cheapest per merchant, totals, coverage Pure functions, unit tested, provider-agnostic
src/api.jsOne request handler, shared by local server and serverless Local and production cannot drift
scripts/probe-coverage.jsThe 9-credit coverage measurement Turns the trial into evidence instead of data

Two product decisions, carried over deliberately

  1. A partial basket is never presented as a cheap basket. In the shipped demo, Costco is the cheapest basket at $18.48 — on 2 of 4 lines. Kroger is $31.63 on all 4. Every card carries its coverage and a filter restricts the view to full-cart stores.
  2. Region is a first-class filter. "US offers only" is on by default; offers with no US evidence are excluded rather than silently blended in.
Why this matters for the budget Every hour spent on cart UI, basket maths, coverage-honest ranking and empty states is permanently reusable, whichever provider wins. The Datafiniti integration is roughly 200 lines. Build the product, rent the data.

7What to do this week

  1. Run the 9-credit probe firstIt costs nothing you will miss and it converts a vendor conversation into a number. Ten minutes.
  2. Then run ten barcode probes on your real SKUsMerchant spread per SKU is the actual constraint on basket comparison. Anything less than three merchants per SKU is not a comparison.
  3. Get §3 answered in writingThe one-sentence question in Section 3. Until that is answered, you have a prototype, not a product.
  4. Decide the category from the dataIf grocery is thin and electronics is dense, ship an electronics cart compare. Smaller, but real, and it is the category a scraped catalogue serves best.
  5. Only then pay $99Pay to serve demand you have observed, not to discover whether demand exists. The trial exists precisely so you never have to buy that answer.
The honest summary Moving to the US turned Datafiniti from "the wrong shape" into a legitimate seed layer for a real product. The remaining risks are commercial and contractual, not technical — which is exactly the right place to be before you spend money.
